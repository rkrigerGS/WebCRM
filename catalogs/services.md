# Service Catalog

The services GovSpring Legal offers, written the way they are actually pitched. This is
what the email engine offers the SA as "which angle do we pitch?" and what it draws the
bridge language from.

Each service has:
- a NAME (what shows in the picker)
- WHEN IT FITS (the prospect signals that make this service relevant; the engine uses
  these to pre-select likely services from a prospect's issue-spotting list)
- HOW IT IS PITCHED (the real framing and vocabulary from approved emails; the engine
  writes in this register, it does not copy verbatim)
- a PROVEN flag where the firm has a demonstrated result worth citing

This is a living document. When an approved email pitches a service in a new way, or
pitches something not listed here, the app proposes an addition for your review.

---

## Overall framing (applies to every email)

The firm's through-line, present in nearly every approved email: GovSpring keeps evolving
regulations from slowing down operations, protects margins, and helps contractors respond
to performance issues before they jeopardize federal business. Legal help is framed as
keeping the work moving, not as crisis response. Tone is warm and congratulatory, never
alarmist. The prospect is doing important work; counsel keeps it on track.

## Small business / SBA compliance

WHEN IT FITS: Company is scaling under a new vehicle or IDIQ. Holds a set-aside. Recently
won a larger award. Operates alongside affiliated entities under shared ownership.

HOW IT IS PITCHED: As firms scale under new vehicles, navigating evolving SBA regulations
becomes critical. "We regularly counsel firms on small business compliance matters."
Affiliation and size are framed as things to get ahead of before a competitor's protest,
not problems they already have: holding a set-aside while operating alongside affiliated
entities under shared ownership tends to raise affiliation and size questions that are far
easier to get ahead of than to defend against.

## 8(a) transition / post-graduation counsel

WHEN IT FITS: Company recently graduated from or is near the end of its 8(a) program term.
Just stood up a JV after graduation.

HOW IT IS PITCHED: Graduation removes sole-source and set-aside protections and creates
transition questions. Framed gently: having experienced government contracts counsel in
your corner can be valuable as the 8(a) term completes.

## Bid protests & corrective action  [PROVEN]

WHEN IT FITS: Company operates in a competitive award environment. Recently won or lost a
contested award. Scaling under a vehicle where protests are common.

HOW IT IS PITCHED: "We have also had recent successes with several bid protests,
strategizing around corrective actions that resulted in at least two direct awards to
clients." This is a demonstrated result and can be stated as such.

## Construction & infrastructure: differing site conditions / REAs  [PROVEN framing]

WHEN IT FITS: Construction, civil works, water/sewer, levee, or infrastructure contract.
Work over aging or decades-old systems. Early in the performance period.

HOW IT IS PITCHED: Subsurface conditions that don't match the as-builts or plans are
exactly where the differing-site-conditions clause and a well-documented request for
equitable adjustment can protect schedule and margin. This is a signature hook for
construction prospects and appears nearly verbatim in multiple approved emails.

## Bonding & certified payroll on large awards

WHEN IT FITS: Larger construction or services award. Public works. Prevailing-wage work.

HOW IT IS PITCHED: The firm helps contractors keep bonding, certified-payroll, and
compliance programs clean on awards of this size, so a contracting officer's questions
never slow the build down. Usually paired with the differing-site-conditions pitch.

## Labor / prevailing wage: Davis-Bacon

WHEN IT FITS: Service or construction contract with a labor component. Federal
construction over threshold. SCA-covered services.

HOW IT IS PITCHED: Certified payroll under Davis-Bacon, alongside broader labor and
compliance support to protect margins and avoid disruption.

## Joint ventures & subcontracting

WHEN IT FITS: Company is part of a JV, especially an ANC/NHO/Tribe-sponsored JV. Recently
formed a JV. Prime on a set-aside performing with subcontractors.

HOW IT IS PITCHED: JV work-share and limitations-on-subcontracting rules, plus
mandatory-disclosure obligations, create a distinctive compliance profile that draws
closer government scrutiny as a build ramps up. Counsel keeps those requirements from
slowing the project.

## Organizational conflicts of interest (OCI)

WHEN IT FITS: Long-running sole-source relationship. Incumbent on unique-knowledge work.
Systems-integration or advisory work near procurement decisions.

HOW IT IS PITCHED: A decades-long sole-source relationship built on unique system
knowledge is exactly the kind of arrangement that draws scrutiny or attention from
competing offerors. OCI questions are far easier to keep clean with counsel on call than
to untangle after a CO or competitor raises them.

## Data rights & IP

WHEN IT FITS: R&D contract. Commercial technology delivered to the government. Space,
defense, or technical development work.

HOW IT IS PITCHED: The firm advises on properly asserting complex data rights so the
government does not overstep when claiming rights to commercial technology.

## Privacy & data handling

WHEN IT FITS: Company administers federal data at scale. Handles policyholder,
beneficiary, or citizen records. DHS or health-agency data.

HOW IT IS PITCHED: Administering federal data at scale brings Privacy Act and DHS
data-handling obligations that reward steady legal attention.

## Drafting / deliverables: codes of conduct  [PROVEN]

WHEN IT FITS: Contract requires a code of conduct or similar governance deliverable. New
prime standing up compliance infrastructure.

HOW IT IS PITCHED: The firm has drafted codes of conduct, including ones required to
perform on a contract. A demonstrated deliverable.

WHEN IT FITS: Company has significant contract exposure but no in-house legal function.
Growing fast. No GC surfaced in research.

HOW IT IS PITCHED: [LIKELY — confirm wording with Marcos] Ongoing counsel on call as a

## Performance issues & compliance (general)

WHEN IT FITS: Any active federal contractor, especially where a specific performance
friction or regulatory requirement is visible.

HOW IT IS PITCHED: The firm helps maintain federal regulatory compliance and strategically
respond to performance issues that may jeopardize federal business. The catch-all when a
more specific service does not dominate.
